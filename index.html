Earlier versions

Version 1.8

13 October 2026. The bleed copies for a commercial printer, corrected after the printer's review. The pages themselves are unchanged from v1.7.
  • The v1.7 bleed copies were wrong. The printer found white strips with a thin line down the middle, and a small colored box in each corner, where the color should have run off the page. The bands now carry the page's own color and photographs the full quarter inch past the edge, so a cut that wanders cannot show white.
  • The crop marks are gone. They sat inside the bleed, so they could show on a wandering cut and they printed on the blank backs.
  • Each bleed file records the finished 8.5 by 11 size inside it, so the printer's software knows where the cut goes and nothing is drawn on the sheet.
  • A new check, tools/check-bleed.py, reads the bleed itself and fails the build if a line of it is not a single color from the sheet edge to the trim. It catches both faults above, and a pale hairline along the right and bottom edges of the faculty sheet that the first fix still left.
  • Every page and filename reads v1.8. The office copier files are the v1.7 pages with the new stamp.

Version 1.7

29 September 2026. A dateless tour card, copies for a commercial printer, a check that catches collisions, and a copy pass against a new writing rubric.
  • The prose was read against a rubric for copy that reads as machine-written, and ten sentences were changed. The flyer names what students actually do with faculty instead of claiming tier-one equipment, and opens on Eric's own sentence about the three majors. The cover carries his paragraph on why technology is not neutral. Page 5 drops the entry-ticket line and leads with what employers want. Page 6 says you prove an algorithm works rather than hope it does, and names who reads the fourth-year project. Page 7 cuts an empty sentence about clubs and says what a mentor is actually for.
  • The rubric itself is in tools/ai-writing-rubric.md, with the eight tells, the scoring, and an explicit list of what is not a tell.
  • No page carries a build date any more. Every stamp reads just the version, v1.7. The set is reprinted whenever a box runs out, and a date made a page that had not changed look stale; the download page is what says which version is current. The model programs still carry "2026-27 catalog", because that tells a family whether the course rows still apply.
  • The folder insert's footer was printing off the bottom of the sheet. The address line ran five points past the page edge and was cut through the middle, and it had been that way since the type on that page was enlarged for v1.6. The page's spacing was tightened to bring the footer back on, and its version stamp now measures from the bottom of the sheet so nothing above it can push it around.
  • Page 7's black footer band was sitting nineteen pixels past the bottom of the sheet, so the Calvin lockup inside it printed cut in half. The band is flush to the edge now, and the room came out of the maroon heading band and the gaps above the photograph, not out of the columns.
  • The build refuses a page whose text runs off the sheet or crowds the edge. The insert's own height guard had been measuring the wrong element and reporting the broken page as clean, so the check now reads the finished PDF instead of trusting the renderer.
  • Every renderer now measures every element on the page, pictures included, against the page box before it writes a PDF, and fails if anything sticks out. A clipped photograph leaves no trace in a finished PDF, which is why page 7 shipped broken twice; this catches it while the browser can still say what it meant to draw.
  • Data Science, on the Data Science Committee's reading: CS 375 joins CS 376 in the junior spring so the pair is taken together; DATA 385 appears twice at two hours each, junior spring and senior fall, because a four-hour section has never been offered; STAT 341, which runs every other spring, replaces STAT 343 as the default and sits in the sophomore spring; and no prerequisite is printed for a course whose prerequisite is already earlier on the same sheet. Still 124 hours, every term between 14 and 16.
  • The three model programs carry Eric's own one-line descriptions at the top right, in place of the drafted ones.
  • The BCS and CS Plus sheets no longer print "MATH 252 needs MATH 251", by the same rule: MATH 251 is already earlier on both sheets. The only prerequisite still printed anywhere is CS 262's "junior standing", which is not a course on the page and so cannot be read off the schedule.
  • The publishing workflow is pinned to ubuntu-24.04 instead of ubuntu-latest, which GitHub moves to Ubuntu 26 on 19 October, and its four actions are on their current major versions.
  • The faculty sheet's version no longer takes a line of the footer band to itself. With the date gone it rides the end of the address line.
  • The download page offers the whole folder in both forms at the top, side by side: the copier file at finished size, and the same sixteen pages with the bleed for a commercial printer. Nobody has to scroll to learn the second one exists.
  • One tour card now, not one per Discovery Day. The route, the talking points and the two fixed facts about every tour are printed; the date, the slot, the co-guides and the group are blank lines to fill in on the day. All three shapes carry the blanks, and nothing needs rebuilding before the next tour.
  • New bleed copies for a commercial printer: the same pages at 9 by 11.5 inches, with a quarter inch of bleed on every side and crop marks at the corners. The office copier files are unchanged.
  • The version stamp has room on every page now. On two pages a paragraph had grown to within a few points of it and read as part of the text.
  • The build checks for collisions: anything printed on top of anything else, and any stamp with less than eight points of clearance, now fails the build instead of reaching print.

Version 1.6

27 September 2026. A copy pass over the whole set after the first Discovery Day, and the words moved out of the layouts.
  • The three majors carry their acronyms on the flyer: BCS, BS in CS and BS in DS.
  • The flyer says graduates can write and communicate in addition to coding, and that every professor brings their faith to bear on teaching and research.
  • The insert reads "one sheet for each degree" and opens its box with "No previous experience in programming is needed!". Its type is larger across the page.
  • The faculty sheet no longer names Discovery Day, so the set serves any recruiting event.
  • Department cover: nobody is "handed off", the doctorate list drops Waterloo, and the degree gives "a breadth of knowledge as well as depth in your field".
  • What you do here: internships become full-time jobs for some students, research is joined "already as an undergraduate student", the core is woven through the undergraduate experience, and the graduate school list gains Waterloo. Alumni who stayed at Calvin have their own sentence, with hymnary.org.
  • More than the code: the opening closes on "the wisdom to know how to shape it", and the six blocks are rearranged so both columns breathe.
  • The tour card for Discovery Day guides is built with the set now, in three shapes: one page, a carry strip, and cards to cut.
  • Every page's text now lives in content/, one plain file per page, so a sentence can be changed without opening a layout.
